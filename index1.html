Physical Principle

The simulation models Stochastic Domain Wall Dynamics. In a perfect crystal, magnetization would be smooth. However, real-world materials have "Impurities" and "Lattice Defects."

  • 1. Pinning Energy: Domain walls get trapped in local energy minima caused by structural defects.
  • 2. Thermal Activation: The simulation assumes the external field (H) provides enough energy to overcome these pinning barriers.
  • 3. Avalanche Effect: One "jump" often triggers nearby domains to flip, creating the "crackle" noise seen in the simulation graphs.

Mathematical Modeling

The simulation calculates the Induced EMF (E) using the rate of change of magnetic flux:

E=−NA(dB/dt)​

Where the total Magnetic Induction (B) is defined by the material response:

B = 𝜇0(H + M)

The Barkhausen Jump Factor:
Each spike represents a discrete change in magnetization ΔM over a tiny time interval Δt:

ΔΦ≈μ0​⋅Vdomain​⋅ΔM
